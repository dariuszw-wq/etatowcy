**If you are not yet 26, your pay from an employment contract or a mandate contract with a company may be exempt from personal income tax (PIT) — up to PLN 85,528 a year.** This is the so-called youth relief, „ulga dla młodych”, often called “zero PIT”. Your employer applies it automatically, without any application from you. It is still worth knowing where the relief ends, because once you exceed the limit or turn 26, the tax comes back.

## What income the relief covers
The PIT exemption covers income from:
- an employment contract, „umowa o pracę” (including outwork and cooperative employment) — also temporary work through an agency,
- a mandate contract, „umowa zlecenia”, concluded with a business, i.e. an entrepreneur, a company or another organisation — including a work agency,
- a graduate internship and a student traineeship,
- maternity allowance, „zasiłek macierzyński”.

The relief does **not** cover, among others, a contract for specific work, „umowa o dzieło”, a mandate contract with a private individual who does not run a business, or income from your own business activity. You pay tax on those earnings under the normal rules.

The law does not make the relief depend on citizenship. What matters is the type of contract, your age and how much you earn. If you are a foreigner, however, the way you are taxed may depend on whether Poland is your country of tax residence and whether there is a double taxation treaty with your country. If in doubt, ask the HR department or the tax office.

## The PLN 85,528 limit — what counts
The limit applies to **income** („przychód”), i.e. the gross amount before deducting costs and contributions, for the whole tax year (January to December). The same limit also includes earnings exempt under other similar reliefs: for returning from abroad, for families with at least four children and for working seniors. So you do not get PLN 85,528 from each relief separately.

Once you exceed the limit, the excess is taxed normally. Earnings up to the limit stay exempt.

Note: the relief applies only to PIT. It does not exempt you from social insurance contributions (ZUS) or the health insurance contribution — separate rules apply to them. That is why “zero PIT” does not mean your net pay equals the gross amount.

## Your 26th birthday — the payment date counts
The law exempts income **received** before you turn 26. What matters is the day you receive the money, not the month it is due for.

Example: your birthday is on 5 December and your employer pays your November salary on 10 December. That payment reaches you after your 26th birthday, so the relief does not apply and a tax advance will be withheld from it.

## When your employer will deduct tax despite the relief
For an employment contract and a mandate contract with a company, the payer (employer or principal) applies the relief automatically. However, you can submit a **request to calculate tax advances without applying the exemption**, „wniosek o obliczanie zaliczek bez stosowania zwolnienia”. This makes sense when:
- you work for several employers and together you will exceed PLN 85,528 — each of them only sees its own payments, so without the request you may owe additional tax at the end of the year,
- you expect to earn above the limit this year and prefer to spread the tax over instalments rather than pay it in one go.

You submit the request in writing or in the way accepted at the company. The employer takes it into account no later than from the month following the month in which it received it. Maternity allowance paid by ZUS is different — there, the relief applies only after you submit a request for it.

If tax advances were withheld even though you were entitled to the exemption, you will get them back in your annual tax return (PIT-37) for that year.

## What this means for you

**If you are an employee or work on a mandate contract:**
- Check your payslip to see whether a PIT advance is being deducted. If you are under 26 and work on an employment or mandate contract with a company, there usually should not be one.
- Keep an eye on your 26th birthday — a payment received after that day is already taxed.
- Working in several places? Add up your total gross earnings for the year. If they may exceed PLN 85,528, consider requesting that tax advances be withheld.
- After the year ends, check the PIT-11 form from each payer and file your PIT-37 — this is how you get back any overpayment or pay any shortfall.

**If you are an employer:**
- Apply the exemption automatically to people under 26 employed on employment and mandate contracts, unless the worker submits a request to withhold tax advances.
- Check the date of birth with every payment — what counts is the day the money is transferred, not the settlement period.
- Monitor the PLN 85,528 limit for payments made by your company in a given year.
- Remember that the relief does not change ZUS or health insurance contributions.
