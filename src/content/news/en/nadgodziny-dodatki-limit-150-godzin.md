**Overtime (praca w godzinach nadliczbowych) is work beyond your working-time norm. An employer may require it only in strictly defined situations, and every overtime hour entitles you to a pay premium or time off.** Below are the key rules of the Labour Code (Kodeks pracy), especially useful if you work shifts or seasonally.

## What counts as overtime
The norm is **8 hours per day and on average 40 hours per week** in the settlement period (okres rozliczeniowy), which as a rule does not exceed 4 months (Art. 129 § 1). The settlement period can be extended to 12 months if objective, technical or organisational reasons justify it (Art. 129 § 2).

Overtime is work beyond the norm that applies to you, as well as work beyond the extended daily working time resulting from your working-time system and schedule (Art. 151 § 1). Where hours are settled over a period, overtime is counted at its end: if you exceed the average weekly norm, each such hour is overtime.

## When your employer may keep you after hours
Overtime is allowed only in the case of (Art. 151 § 1):
- **a rescue operation** to protect life, health, property or the environment, or to remove a breakdown,
- **special needs of the employer**.

Limits:
- overtime for "special needs" may not exceed **150 hours per calendar year** per employee (Art. 151 § 3). A different limit may follow from a collective agreement, the work rules, or the employment contract if the employer has neither an agreement nor work rules (Art. 151 § 4),
- weekly working time **including overtime may not exceed 48 hours on average** in the settlement period (Art. 131 § 1).

Overtime **must not** be ordered for, among others, a pregnant employee (Art. 178 § 1) or a juvenile worker (Art. 203 § 1). An employee raising a child under 8 may be given overtime only with their consent (Art. 178 § 2).

## How much you get for overtime
Besides normal pay, you receive a premium (dodatek) (Art. 151¹ § 1):
- **100%** of pay — for overtime at night, on Sundays and public holidays that are not working days for you, and on a day off given in exchange for work on a Sunday or holiday,
- **50%** of pay — for overtime on any other day.

The 100% premium also applies to overtime resulting from exceeding the average weekly norm in the settlement period (Art. 151¹ § 2), unless that excess results from overtime for which a premium is already due.

**Example (illustrative amounts):** if your hourly rate based on your personal grade is PLN 40 gross, an overtime hour on an ordinary day pays PLN 40 + PLN 20 premium = PLN 60 gross. At night or on a Sunday — PLN 40 + PLN 40 = PLN 80 gross. If the contract does not separate the personal-grade component, the premium is calculated from 60% of pay (Art. 151¹ § 3).

## Time off instead of the premium
- At your request (paper or electronic), the employer may give you **the same number of hours off** as you worked beyond the norm. No premium is then paid (Art. 151² § 1 and 3).
- The employer may also grant time off **without your request**, by the end of the settlement period at the latest, but **one and a half times** the overtime worked (e.g. 15 hours off for 10 overtime hours). It must not reduce your pay for the full monthly working time (Art. 151² § 2).
- If you worked on a day off under your schedule because of a rescue operation or breakdown, you are entitled to another day off by the end of the settlement period (Art. 151³).

If your employment ends before the settlement period is over and you worked more than the norm up to that date, you are also entitled to the premium (Art. 151⁶).

## What this means for you
**If you are an employee:**
- Keep your own record of start and finish times and compare it with your payslip at the end of the settlement period.
- Check whether your contract contains a "lump sum for overtime" — a lump sum is allowed only for permanent work outside the workplace, and its amount must match the expected overtime (Art. 151¹ § 4).
- If you are not paid, raise it with your employer first, then you can ask the National Labour Inspectorate (PIP, Państwowa Inspekcja Pracy) for help or go to the labour court.

**If you are an employer:**
- Order overtime only in the situations in Art. 151 § 1 and watch the 150-hour limit and the 48-hour weekly average.
- Separate exceptions apply to heads of separate units and persons managing the workplace (Art. 151⁴) — do not apply them automatically to everyone.
- Document requests for time off and the hours granted to avoid disputes over premiums.
