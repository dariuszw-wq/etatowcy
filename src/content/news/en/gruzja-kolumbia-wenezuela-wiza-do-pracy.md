**Citizens of Georgia, Colombia and Venezuela can no longer come to Poland to work on their passport alone. Since 15 August 2026 entry in connection with work requires a visa, and since 22 August 2026 a visa-free stay is not enough to work on the basis of a work permit (zezwolenie na pracę).** These are two separate changes, introduced by two regulations. We explain who they affect and what to do if you are already in Poland.

## Change 1: a visa to enter for work (since 15 August 2026)
Visa-free travel (entry without a visa, on a biometric passport alone) normally lets you stay in the Schengen area for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. A regulation of the Minister of Foreign Affairs introduced an exception to this rule. Citizens of:
- Georgia,
- Colombia,
- Venezuela

who **enter Poland in connection with being entrusted with work** must have a visa (§ 2 and § 3 of the regulation).

In the Act, “entrusting work” (powierzenie pracy) is a very broad term. It covers not only an employment contract (umowa o pracę) but also, among others, a **contract of mandate (umowa zlecenia), a contract for services (umowa o świadczenie usług), a contract for specific work (umowa o dzieło)** and help with harvesting (Art. 2(9) of the Act on the conditions for entrusting work to foreigners). So you need a visa regardless of the type of contract.

Tourists and people visiting family can still enter without a visa — the exception applies only to entry in connection with work.

**If you entered before 15 August 2026** — the previous rules apply to you (§ 4 of the regulation).

## Change 2: no work on a permit while visa-free (since 22 August 2026)
The second regulation, issued by the Minister of Family, Labour and Social Policy, provides that citizens of Venezuela, Georgia and Colombia **are not entitled to work on the basis of a work permit** while staying in Poland visa-free (§ 1). A work permit from the voivode alone is not enough — you need another basis of stay, e.g. a visa or a residence permit.

**Transitional rule (§ 2):** if, during a visa-free stay and holding a work permit, **you started work before 22 August 2026**, you may continue on the previous terms — but only until your visa-free stay ends. The Ministry stresses that this rule does not protect people who were only planning to work or who obtained a permit later.

## If you applied for a temporary residence and work permit
According to the Ministry of Family, Labour and Social Policy (PSZ portal, 20.08.2026), people who **before 22 August 2026** held a valid work permit or a declaration on entrusting work (oświadczenie o powierzeniu pracy) may work while waiting for the decision on the temporary residence and work permit (zezwolenie na pobyt czasowy i pracę). Those who had no such document before cannot work on this basis while waiting.

## Note: Georgia no longer uses declarations
Since 1 December 2025 Georgia has not been on the list of countries whose citizens can work on the basis of a **declaration on entrusting work to a foreigner (oświadczenie o powierzeniu pracy cudzoziemcowi)** (regulation Dz.U. 2025 item 1617). This simplified route now covers citizens of Armenia, Belarus, Moldova and Ukraine. A Georgian citizen therefore, as a rule, needs a work permit or a temporary residence and work permit.

## What this means for you

**If you are a citizen of Georgia, Colombia or Venezuela:**
- Planning to come to work? First apply for a visa at a Polish consulate. Without a visa you do not meet the conditions for entry for work.
- Are you in Poland without a visa and holding a work permit? Check the date you started work and the date you entered. They determine whether you can keep working and until when.
- Do not start work “on trial” without the required documents. Work that your basis of stay does not allow is illegal and may harm your future residence cases.
- If in doubt, call the Zielona Linia helpline (19524) or consult an adviser.

**If you are an employer:**
- Before hiring a citizen of Georgia, Colombia or Venezuela, check their **basis of stay** (visa, residence card, passport stamp) and **date of entry** — a work permit alone is not enough.
- Keep a copy of the residence document in the employee's records.
- Do not use the declaration on entrusting work when hiring citizens of these countries — that route does not cover them.
- Entrusting work to a person whose basis of stay does not allow work exposes you to sanctions (this is “illegal entrusting of work” within the meaning of Art. 2(2)(b) of the Act).

*Legal basis: Regulation of the Minister of Family, Labour and Social Policy of 31 July 2026 on the list of countries whose citizens holding a work permit are not entitled to work during a visa-free stay in the Republic of Poland (Dz.U. 2026 item 1072) — in force since 22 August 2026; Regulation of the Minister of Foreign Affairs of 10 August 2026 establishing exceptions to the visa exemption (Dz.U. 2026 item 1086) — exception applied since 15 August 2026; Act of 20 March 2025 on the conditions for entrusting work to foreigners in the Republic of Poland (Dz.U. 2025 item 621 as amended), Art. 2(2) and 2(9), Art. 3(4); MRPiPS regulation (Dz.U. 2025 item 1617); MRPiPS information on the PSZ portal of 20 August 2026. Legal status as of 5 October 2026.*
