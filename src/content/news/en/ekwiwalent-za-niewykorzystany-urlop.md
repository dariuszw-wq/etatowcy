**If your employment contract ends and you have not used all your annual leave, your employer must pay you a cash equivalent for those days — in Polish, *ekwiwalent pieniężny za urlop*.** The end of the year is when many fixed-term contracts expire. Check how much you should receive and by when.

## When you are entitled to the equivalent
You are entitled to it when you have not used your leave in full or in part **because the employment relationship was terminated or expired** (Art. 171 § 1 of the Labour Code, *Kodeks pracy*). It does not matter who gave notice or whether the contract simply ran out.

As long as you are employed, your employer cannot "buy out" your leave. Money instead of days off only comes into play when the contract ends.

There are two exceptions:
- **A new contract with the same employer.** If you sign another contract with the same company right after the previous one ends, the parties may agree that you will use your leave under the new contract. In that case there is no cash equivalent (Art. 171 § 3).
- **Leave during the notice period.** Your employer may grant you leave during your notice period (*okres wypowiedzenia*), and you must then take it (Art. 167¹). Days you use are not paid out as an equivalent.

## How many leave days you have left
In the year your contract ends, your leave is **proportional to the time you worked for that employer** (Art. 155¹ § 1 point 1). A part of a day is rounded up to a full day (Art. 155³ § 1).

Example: you are entitled to 26 days of leave a year and your contract ends on 30 June 2026. For half a year you are owed 13 days. If you have used 8, you will receive the equivalent for 5 days.

Add any outstanding leave from the previous year (*urlop zaległy*) that you have not used yet.

## How to calculate the equivalent step by step
The rules are set out in the leave regulation (§ 18). The calculation is done in hours:

1. **Divide your monthly pay by the coefficient.** This gives the equivalent for 1 day of leave.
2. **Divide the result by your daily working-time norm.** For a full-time job this is usually 8 hours. This gives the equivalent for 1 hour.
3. **Multiply by the number of hours of unused leave.** 1 day of leave is usually 8 hours.

Monthly pay includes your fixed salary for the month in which you became entitled to the equivalent (§ 15). Variable components, such as monthly bonuses or overtime pay, are taken as an average of the previous 3 months (§ 16). Components paid for periods longer than a month, such as a quarterly bonus, are taken as an average of 12 months (§ 17).

## The coefficient in 2026 and 2027: 20.92
The coefficient is calculated separately for each year (§ 19). Formula: 365 days minus Sundays, public holidays and days off resulting from a five-day working week. The result is divided by 12.

In both 2026 and 2027 the result is the same: **251 working days, i.e. a coefficient of 20.92** (our calculation from the calendar based on § 19(2)). You use the coefficient for the year in which you became entitled to the equivalent, i.e. the year your contract ends.

Working part-time? The coefficient is reduced proportionally (§ 19(3)). For a half-time job it is 10.46.

**Example for the minimum wage (full-time, 5 days = 40 hours of leave):**

| Contract ends | Gross monthly pay | Per 1 day | Per 1 hour | For 40 hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 | PLN 4806 | PLN 229.73 | PLN 28.72 | **PLN 1148.80 gross** |
| 2027 | PLN 4950 | PLN 236.62 | PLN 29.58 | **PLN 1183.20 gross** |

Amounts rounded to the nearest grosz. These are gross amounts, from which contributions and income tax advances are deducted.

## Payment deadline and limitation period
Since 27 January 2026 the Labour Code states explicitly when the employer must pay:
- **On the regular payday** applicable in the company (Art. 171 § 4).
- **Within 10 days of the end of the contract**, if the payday falls before the date the contract ends. If that tenth day is a day off work, the money must be paid one day earlier (Art. 171 § 5).

A claim for the equivalent becomes time-barred after **3 years** from the date it became due (Art. 291 § 1).

## Temporary workers
If you work through a temporary work agency (*agencja pracy tymczasowej*), you are entitled to **2 days of leave for each month** of work for the user employer (Art. 17(1) of the Temporary Employment Act). The agency pays out unused leave as an equivalent (Art. 17(3)). It does not have to do so if you sign another contract with it to work for the same user employer and the parties agree that you will use your leave under that contract (Art. 17(3a)). In all other matters the Labour Code and the leave regulation apply (Art. 5 of the Act).

## What this means for you
**If you are an employee:**
- Before your contract ends, check with HR how many leave days you have left, including outstanding leave.
- Check your payslip to see whether the equivalent was calculated for the correct number of hours.
- If your employer does not pay on time, you can contact the National Labour Inspectorate (*Państwowa Inspekcja Pracy*, PIP) or the labour court.

**If you are an employer:**
- For contracts ending on 31 December, plan to pay the equivalent together with salaries or within 10 days of the end of the contract.
- If you want to keep employing the same person, agree in writing that the leave carries over to the new contract.
- For part-time employees, reduce the coefficient in proportion to their working time.
