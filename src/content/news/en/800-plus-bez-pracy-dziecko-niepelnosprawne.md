**A mother of three: a disabled daughter and two healthy sons. ZUS granted 800+ for the daughter and refused it for the sons — because they hold no disability certificate. The Regional Administrative Court in Kraków quashed those decisions and called that reading absurd.** Courts in Białystok and Poznań ruled the same way. If you are out of work because you care for a disabled child, this concerns you directly.

## Where the work condition came from

An amendment of 12 September 2025 added a new Art. 1(2a) to the Act on state aid in raising children. It took effect on **1 February 2026** and is brief: the child-raising benefit for a given month is due to a foreign national if **in the preceding month they were professionally active in Poland**.

The condition covers the foreign nationals listed in Art. 1(2)(2)(c)–(f) — among them holders of a **residence card with the annotation "access to the labour market"** (*dostęp do rynku pracy*), holders of a temporary residence permit under Art. 127 of the Act on Foreigners (EU Blue Card), intra-corporate transferees and researchers. It does not cover Polish citizens or people covered by the coordination of social security systems, including EU citizens.

One date is easily missed: under the transitional provision the new rules apply **for the first time when establishing the right to the benefit for the period beginning 1 June 2026**. Earlier periods are governed by the previous rules. For people with UKR status a separate rule moved that moment to 5 March 2026.

## What the Act counts as professional activity

This is not the everyday sense of "I work". The Act has its own definition (Art. 2(1a)), and that is what decides:

| Basis | Condition |
|---|---|
| Employment contract, mandate contract, agency contract and similar | the pension-contribution base **not lower than 50% of the minimum wage** |
| Business activity | no 50% threshold; for benefits the threshold is 30% of the minimum wage |
| Parental (childcare) leave | being covered by pension insurance on that basis |
| Maternity, sickness and carer's allowance, rehabilitation benefit | subject to the 50% threshold (or 30% for business activity) |
| Old-age or disability pension, supplementary parental benefit, support benefit | being covered by health insurance on that basis |
| Farmers' insurance (KRUS), voluntary insurance under Art. 7 of the Social Insurance Act | yes |

The practical upshot: **half-time work at the minimum rate may not be enough** if the contribution base falls below half the minimum wage. What counts is the figure on the ZUS declaration, not the wording of the contract.

## Three exceptions — and a dispute over how to read them

The second sentence of Art. 1(2a) switches off the work condition where the **child** is a Polish citizen, holds a disability certificate or a certificate of a moderate or severe degree of disability, or is in the care of a guardian appointed by the authorities of the country of origin.

That is where the trouble starts. The provision speaks of the child for whom the parent applies. ZUS read it literally: the exception belongs to **that particular child** who holds the certificate. Siblings without one were to go without the benefit, because the parent does not work.

## What the courts held

**Regional Administrative Court in Kraków, judgment of 4 August 2026 (III SA/Kr 796/26).** The court quashed the ZUS decisions refusing 800+ for the two healthy sons. It held that reading the exemption as applying only to the disabled child contradicts the wording of the provision, its logic and its purpose. It produces an absurd result: a mother who is out of work not by choice but because she must care for a disabled child is **punished by losing the benefit for her other children**. If even one child in the parent's care holds a certificate, the exemption extends to the siblings.

**Regional Administrative Court in Białystok, two judgments of 24 September 2026 (II SA/Bk 442/26 and 443/26).** The court went further and looked at the mother's own status. It noted that for a person receiving the carer's benefit (*świadczenie pielęgnacyjne*) the municipal authority pays pension contributions, and that person is covered by health insurance. She is therefore within the insurance system continuously and compulsorily — and that, in the court's view, should be treated as professional activity.

It is worth knowing why that argument was needed at all. Contributions for a person on the carer's benefit are paid under **Art. 6(2a) of the Social Insurance Act**, while the definition of professional activity refers to listed points of Art. 6(1). Care for a disabled child simply **did not make it onto the statutory list**. The court filled the gap by purposive interpretation.

**Regional Administrative Court in Poznań, judgment of 24 September 2026 (III SA/Po 630/26).** Here the mother received the carer's benefit for her disabled husband under the "old rules", which rule out taking up work. ZUS refused 800+ for the child. The court held that since receiving the carer's benefit for the care of a disabled child is no obstacle to the benefit, treating people on the "old" carer's benefit differently has no legal justification.

## What these judgments do not settle

This has to be said plainly: **these are rulings in individual cases and they do not bind ZUS in your case.** There is no Supreme Administrative Court resolution and no change to the provision. The organisations that raised the problem back at the legislative stage — the Ukrainian House Foundation and the Legal Intervention Association — point out that only an amendment clarifying the provision would settle it for good.

The practical conclusion, though, is not the one many people draw: **a refusal is worth appealing.** Many foreign nationals, on hearing "you do not qualify", file nothing at all. These three cases show what the opposite decision leads to.

## What this means for you

**If you are a foreign national:**
- Check whether the work condition applies to you at all. EU citizens and people covered by social security coordination fall outside it. What matters is your basis of stay, not citizenship as such.
- Do you have a child with **Polish citizenship**? The work condition falls away — this exception is often overlooked, and it covers many families.
- A refusal for healthy siblings alongside a disabled child is exactly the situation three courts found wrong. **Do not leave that decision unappealed.**
- The disability certificate must be **Polish** — certificates issued abroad are not automatically recognised by Polish authorities.
- Check the contribution base on your ZUS declaration. With part-time work it is that figure, not the contract, that decides whether the 50% threshold is met.
- Not working and outside the exceptions? Registering as unemployed gives the benefit for 3 months, or 6 with three or more children — but only **once per benefit period**.
- Remember the condition unrelated to work: the child must be in pre-school, compulsory schooling or education, which ZUS verifies in the Education Information System.

**If you employ foreign nationals:**
- A drop in working hours or a break in employment in one month can cut off an employee's 800+ in the next. Worth flagging when changing terms of employment.
- On mandate contracts, watch the contribution base — the 50% threshold of the minimum wage is a real line here.
- ZUS may suspend payment where there is doubt about residence in Poland, and it receives border-crossing data from the Border Guard. Longer trips abroad matter here.
