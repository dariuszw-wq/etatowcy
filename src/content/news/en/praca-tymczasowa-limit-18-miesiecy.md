**Have you been working at the same company through a temporary work agency for many months? The law limits this time: no more than 18 months in total within 36 consecutive months.** The limit applies both to the agency that assigns you and to the company where you work. It is worth knowing how to count it — before exceeding it surprises you or your employer.

## Who the limit applies to
Temporary work involves three parties:
- **the temporary work agency** (agencja pracy tymczasowej) — employs you and pays your wages,
- **the user employer** (pracodawca użytkownik) — the company where you actually work and which assigns your tasks,
- **you** — the temporary worker (pracownik tymczasowy).

Art. 20 of the Act on the employment of temporary workers provides that:
1. the agency may assign you to one user employer for a period **not exceeding 18 months in total within 36 consecutive months**,
2. the user employer may use your work for **the same maximum period**.

The limit covers work under both an **employment contract** (umowa o pracę) and a **civil-law contract** (e.g. umowa zlecenia — a contract of mandate), if you were assigned by a temporary work agency.

## How to count 18 months in 36
The limit is counted **in total** — breaks do not reset it. If you worked at company X through an agency for 10 months, then had a break, and later came back for 8 months, you have already used the whole 18-month limit within the 36-month period.

The 36-month period means **36 consecutive months** — each time you check how long in total you have worked for the given company during the last 36 months.

Important: the law also ties the limit to the **user employer**. Moving to a different agency does not give you a new 18 months at the same company — the company can still use your temporary work for only 18 months in total within 36.

## Exception: replacing an absent employee
You can work longer if you **continuously replace an absent employee** of that company (e.g. a person on long sick leave or parental leave). The limit is then **up to 36 months**. After such a period, the same agency may not assign you to the same company, and the company may not take you on as a temporary worker, for the next **36 months**.

The limits also do not apply when the contract of a pregnant temporary worker is extended until the day of childbirth (Art. 177 § 3 of the Labour Code).

## Penalties for exceeding the limit
Exceeding the limit is a petty offence. A **fine of PLN 1,000 to PLN 30,000** may be imposed on:
- a person acting on behalf of the agency that assigns a worker beyond the limit (Art. 27a),
- a person acting on behalf of the user employer that uses the work beyond the limit (Art. 27b).

The request for a penalty is filed by a labour inspector of the National Labour Inspectorate (Państwowa Inspekcja Pracy, PIP).

## Other temporary work rules worth knowing
- The agency employs temporary workers under a **fixed-term employment contract**. The general Labour Code limit of 33 months and three fixed-term contracts does not apply here.
- The contract may provide for shorter notice: **3 days** (contract of up to 2 weeks) or **1 week** (contract longer than 2 weeks).
- You may not be treated less favourably than the company's employees in the same or a similar position — as regards working conditions and other terms of employment.
- You are entitled to leave: **2 days for each month** of being at the disposal of a user employer.

## What this means for you

**If you are a temporary worker:**
- Write down the start and end dates of your work at each company — they will be useful when the limit needs to be counted.
- When you get close to 18 months, ask the agency what comes next: an assignment to another company or being hired directly by the company where you work (then no longer as a temporary worker).
- If you are a foreigner, any change of the entity you work for may require checking your work permit (zezwolenie na pracę) or declaration (oświadczenie) — do this before the change, at the agency or at the office.

**If you are a user employer:**
- Keep your own record of the working periods of each temporary worker — you are responsible for the limit regardless of the agency, also when the worker has changed agencies.
- In the case of a replacement, state in your arrangements with the agency whom the worker is replacing — this is the basis for the longer, 36-month period.
- If you want to keep a proven worker for longer, plan to hire them directly in advance.
